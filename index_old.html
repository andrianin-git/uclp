The invisible cost of inaction

One observation that cuts across all seven failure modes: the risk of innovating is documented; the risk of delay usually is not.
!

Doing nothing is also a decision.

Approving a new technology creates an identifiable owner and a visible risk. Delaying or refusing it can feel safer, even when the benefit patients miss out on builds up over months or years.

What the lunches revealed

Information governance and procurement staff can block proposals without being responsible for the clinical consequences of delay. Participants described innovations delayed for years, and clinically useful technologies reaching some trusts decades later than others.

Question for UCLP

“What would change if every decision to delay an innovation included an explicit assessment of the cost of waiting?”
